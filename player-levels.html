In detail

What each level looks like

Beginner Padel Australia 1.0 – 1.5

For players who are completely new to padel or have only played a handful of times.

You are learning the basic rules, scoring, court positioning and fundamental shots. Serving, returning and maintaining a rally are still developing, and you may have limited experience playing the ball after it rebounds from the glass.

You belong here if: you're still learning the game and developing the basic skills needed to comfortably play a match.

Beginner + Padel Australia 1.5 – 2.0

For players who have moved beyond their first few games and understand the basics of how padel is played.

You can serve, return and participate in rallies, particularly at a slower pace. You're developing greater consistency and beginning to understand doubles positioning, moving towards the net and using the glass.

You belong here if: you understand the game and can play points and short rallies, but you're still developing consistency, positioning and confidence.

Beginner ++ Padel Australia 2.0 – 2.5

For regular beginner players who are becoming comfortable playing full matches.

You can consistently keep the ball in play at a slow to medium pace and have greater control over your serve, return and groundstrokes. You're beginning to use volleys, overheads and the glass as part of your game and have a better understanding of doubles positioning.

Accuracy and consistency may still decrease when rallies become quicker or you're placed under pressure.

You belong here if: you can comfortably play a full match and maintain rallies, but you're still developing accuracy, shot selection, glass play and consistency at higher speeds.

Intermediate Padel Australia 3.0 – 3.5

For established players who can play confidently and consistently at medium pace.

You have a good understanding of doubles positioning, can defend using the glass and are comfortable transitioning between the back of the court and the net. Your volleys and overheads are becoming reliable, and you're beginning to construct points rather than simply keeping the ball in play.

You can maintain consistency as rallies become quicker and more competitive.

You belong here if: you're comfortable in all areas of the court, understand positioning and basic tactics, and can consistently compete with other experienced club players.

Intermediate Advanced Padel Australia 3.5 – 4.0

For strong and experienced club players who can play consistently at a faster pace.

You are confident defending with the glass, have effective net play and can use volleys and overheads reliably. You understand how to construct points with your partner, anticipate play and transition effectively between defensive and attacking positions.

At the upper end of this level, players can create winners, force errors and maintain control during faster, more competitive rallies.

You belong here if: you're a strong intermediate player who can compete consistently at higher speeds, construct points tactically and confidently use a broad range of attacking and defensive shots.

Where do I fit?

Most players sit within these five

The Padel Logan Player Level Guide is built for the majority of social and club players, from complete beginners through to strong, experienced intermediates.

The Padel Australia Player Rating Guide continues beyond these levels into Advanced, which generally applies to highly experienced competitive and tournament players. As there are currently fewer players at that standard, we do not run a separate Advanced category. Advanced players are of course welcome — dedicated sessions may not always be available, but you can book a court and organise matches against players of a similar standard.

Not sure which level you are? Start with the one that best describes your current ability. If you're regularly one of the strongest players in your group, it may be time to move up.
